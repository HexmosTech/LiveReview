Which chart answers which kind of question. This chapter is sent to both
Planning and Finalizing: Planning needs it to know what shape the data
must arrive in, Finalizing needs it to know how to draw what arrived.

Every chart described here is a Vega-Lite specification — the allowed
marks, the flat/layered/faceted shapes a spec can take, and the field-type
rules are the Chart Grammar section immediately below, read before any of
the families that follow.

The sections are organized by the comparison the question asks for, not by
its topic. That distinction is the one that decides correctly: velocity
appears under trend, comparison and relationship alike, and what separates
them is whether the reader wants a movement over time, a before-and-after,
or a correlation.

Each family states its governing rule first, then the situations it covers.
An exception displaces the rule it sits under, and exists precisely where
the general mechanism would conceal the thing being asked about.
